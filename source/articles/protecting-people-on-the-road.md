# Protecting people: move over and community safety zones

**Covers:** Move over law, Community safety zone

## People on foot and at work {#overview}
Some people on the road are far more exposed than you are, sitting inside a car: a tow truck operator on the shoulder, a paramedic helping a patient, a child walking to school. Ontario has two rules that give them extra space and set higher penalties for drivers who ignore them. Let's look at each one.

## Move over law {#move-over-law}
The move over law applies when you approach a stopped emergency vehicle, tow truck or maintenance vehicle with its lights flashing. You must slow down and, if there is more than one lane going your way and it is safe, move into the next lane to leave a gap.

What if you cannot move over, because the other lane is full or there is only one lane? Then slow down and be ready to stop. Do not speed up past the vehicle. The reason is this: a worker standing beside a truck on the shoulder is only a metre or two from traffic, and a vehicle passing at highway speed can be fatal. Fines are high, and the rule exists to give workers room.

## Community safety zone {#community-safety-zone}
A community safety zone is a stretch of road where public safety is a special concern, such as near a school, a daycare, a hospital or a seniors' residence. Signs mark the start and the end, and the speed limit may be lower.

Fines for traffic offences inside the zone are doubled. This applies to speeding and also to other offences, such as failing to stop at a crossing. The higher penalty applies whenever the signs are posted, even if no children are visible at that moment. The zone is there to change habits, and not only to catch people at busy times.

## A worked example {#example}
You are driving on a two-lane road in a community safety zone near a school, at 40 km/h in a posted 40 zone. A tow truck is stopped ahead with its lights flashing. Let's go through it.

1. Notice the school sign and the posted 40 km/h limit. Keep your speed there or lower.
2. See the flashing lights early and take your foot off the accelerator.
3. Check the mirror and the next lane. If the road has two lanes in your direction and the left lane is clear, signal and move over.
4. If there is only one lane, slow down enough that you could stop if the operator stepped out.
5. Return to your lane when you are well past, and signal again.

A speeding fine in the zone of, for example, 150 dollars would become 300. The figures are only an example. Check the real fine schedule for exact amounts.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
A common slip is assuming the law covers only police cars and ambulances. It covers tow trucks and other vehicles with flashing lights too.

A second slip is treating the zone's lower limit as optional when you cannot see anyone around.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Slow down and move over a lane for stopped emergency, tow and maintenance vehicles with flashing lights, when it is safe.
- If you cannot move over, slow down and be prepared to stop.
- A community safety zone is marked by signs, and fines for traffic offences inside it are doubled.
- Both rules protect people who are exposed to traffic, so give them space.
