# Blind spots and shoulder checks

**Covers:** Blind spot, Shoulder check

## What the mirrors do not show {#overview}
Mirrors are useful, but they are flat windows onto one angle of the road. A car or a cyclist can sit just outside what they show, even when you check them carefully. Many lane-change crashes happen because a driver looked in the mirror, saw nothing, and moved over.

This article explains the gap in what you can see and the quick check that closes it.

## Blind spot {#blind-spot}
A blind spot is an area around your car that you cannot see in the mirrors or straight ahead. On most cars there is one on each side, beside and slightly behind the rear door pillar. A smaller one sits directly behind the car, and in large vehicles it is much bigger.

A vehicle can travel at your speed inside your blind spot for a long time, so it never appears in the mirror. Motorcycles and bicycles are small enough to hide in it easily. You can reduce it by setting your side mirrors a little wider than usual, but you cannot remove it.

## Shoulder check {#shoulder-check}
A shoulder check is a quick glance over your shoulder, in the direction you plan to go, to look into the blind spot. It takes under a second. Turn your head, not your whole body, and keep your hands steady on the wheel.

Do it every time before you change lanes, before you pull away from the curb, and before a turn where a cyclist could be beside you. Do it after your mirror check and signal, and just before you start to move, because the situation can change in between.

## A worked example {#example}
You are on a two-lane road and want to move into the left lane to pass a slow truck.

1. Check the rear-view mirror for traffic behind.
2. Check the left side mirror.
3. Turn on your left signal early, so others know your plan.
4. Do the shoulder check to the left. This is where you may see a car you missed.
5. If the lane is clear, move over smoothly, keeping your speed steady.
6. Turn off your signal.

At step 4, suppose a small car is travelling next to you. The mirror did not show it. Because you looked, you stay in your lane and avoid a collision. The whole routine takes about four seconds.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Doing the shoulder check too early or too long. If you glance over your shoulder five seconds before moving, a car can arrive in the space afterwards. If you look for too long, the car drifts. Make it quick and make it the last look before you move. A second mistake is relying only on blind-spot warning lights if the car has them: they help, but they can miss fast or small vehicles.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Every car has blind spots that mirrors cannot show.
- A shoulder check is a short look over your shoulder into the blind spot.
- Do it before every lane change, turn or pull-away, as the last look before you move.
- Mirrors, signal, then shoulder check, in that order.
