# Freeways and merging

**Covers:** Freeway, Merge

## Driving where nothing stops you {#overview}
A freeway is built for steady, fast traffic. There are no cross streets, no stop signs and no traffic lights, so everything depends on drivers fitting in smoothly. Most of the stress for new drivers comes at the two moments when you join and leave the flow. Preparing for those moments makes the rest of the trip much easier.

## Freeway {#freeway}
A freeway is a divided highway: opposing traffic is separated by a median or barrier, and you enter and leave only by ramps. Because there are no intersections, speeds are high, and that raises the cost of any mistake.

A few habits help. Keep a following distance of at least two seconds and add more as speed rises. Stay in the right lane unless you are passing. Look far ahead, so that you notice slowing traffic early. Plan your exit in advance and move toward it several kilometres before, not at the last moment. Remember that G1 drivers may not use the fastest highways at all, so check the rules for your licence level.

## Merge {#merge}
To merge is to join moving traffic from a ramp or an ending lane. The goal is to arrive at the speed of the traffic you are joining, not at the speed you were doing on the ramp.

Use the ramp, which is the acceleration lane, to speed up. Signal, check your mirror and do a shoulder check for the space beside you, then slide into a gap. Never stop at the end of the ramp unless traffic is blocked. A stopped car cannot match speed, and the cars behind you may be unable to stop.

Traffic already on the freeway should help by moving over or adjusting speed when it is safe, but the driver who is merging must give way if there is no safe gap. Zipper merging, where two lanes take turns one by one, works when lanes end in a queue.

## A worked example {#example}
You are entering a freeway where traffic travels at 100 km/h.

1. On the ramp, look at the mirror and over your shoulder to find a gap. Check the signs for the ramp's length.
2. Signal early, then accelerate along the ramp. By the end, you should be at about 100 km/h.
3. Find a gap of at least three seconds between two cars: at 100 km/h, three seconds is about 83 metres.
4. Do a shoulder check, then move over smoothly.
5. Cancel the signal and adjust your gap to the car ahead until it is at least two seconds.

If no gap is available, slow down a little and keep your speed at the end of the ramp. Do not squeeze in.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Driving slowly on the ramp because the freeway looks scary. A slow merge forces other drivers to brake, which causes the very crashes you want to avoid. The second common mistake is changing lanes repeatedly to get ahead. Every extra lane change is another chance for a collision.

## Key points {#key-points}
- A freeway has no intersections, so speed is high and smooth driving is important.
- Merge by using the ramp to match the speed of traffic, signalling, and doing a shoulder check.
- Do not stop or crawl on the ramp unless traffic forces it.
- Plan your exit early and keep a safe gap.
