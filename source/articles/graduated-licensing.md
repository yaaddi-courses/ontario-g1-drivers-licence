# How graduated licensing works in Ontario

**Covers:** Graduated licensing, G1, G2, Beginner driver education, BDE

## Why new drivers get a ladder {#overview}
New drivers are the group most likely to crash, mostly because they have little experience judging speed, gaps and hazards. Ontario's answer is not to block them from driving. It lets them build experience in steps, with strict limits at the start that ease as they prove themselves.

This article explains the steps, the rules at the first two, and how a driving course can shorten the wait.

## Graduated licensing {#graduated-licensing}
The system has three levels in order: G1, then G2, then the full G licence. Each step has its own rules about who may sit beside you, when you may drive, which roads you may use and how much alcohol is allowed in your blood. To move up, you pass a test and wait out a minimum time.

The idea is a safety net: the riskiest conditions, such as fast highways at night, stay closed until you have more hours behind the wheel.

## G1 {#g1}
G1 is the first level and is a learner licence. You get it by passing the written knowledge test, which is what this course prepares you for.

The main G1 limits are:
- You must drive with an accompanying driver who has at least four years of experience and a full licence.
- Your blood alcohol must be zero.
- You must not drive between midnight and 5 a.m.
- You must stay off the fastest highways, the 400-series roads with a posted limit above 80 km/h.

G1 is meant for learning, so the rules assume someone experienced is sitting next to you.

## G2 {#g2}
G2 is the second level, reached by passing a road test at the end of G1. You may drive alone, but you are still a novice.

The rules are fewer, not gone. Your blood alcohol must still be zero. If you are 19 or under, limits on passengers apply late at night, from midnight to 5 a.m. After a period at G2, you take a second road test to earn the full G licence. Check the Ministry of Transportation website for the current passenger numbers, because they can change.

## Beginner driver education {#beginner-driver-education}
Beginner driver education is an approved course of at least 40 hours, usually with a driving school. It covers classroom lessons and in-car practice with an instructor.

The reward is time. A normal G1 lasts twelve months, but finishing an approved course lets you take the road test after eight months. A school may also lower your insurance cost, so ask your insurer.

## BDE {#bde}
BDE is the short form you will see on forms and websites. It means the same thing as beginner driver education. When a driving school says "BDE certificate", it is the proof you need to claim the shorter wait, so keep it safe.

## A worked example {#example}
Two learners start G1 on the same day. Sam does not take the course. Nina completes an approved 40-hour course in the first two months.

| | Sam | Nina |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum G1 wait | 12 months | 8 months |
| First road test possible | After month 12 | After month 8 |
| Difference | | 4 months earlier |

Nina still has to pass the road test. The course does not give a licence. It only shortens the waiting time.

## A common mistake {#mistake}
Thinking that a course replaces the road test: it only shortens the wait. Another mistake is driving alone "just around the corner". At G1, driving without an experienced companion breaks the rules, however short the trip.

## Key points {#key-points}
- Graduated licensing gives new drivers more freedom step by step: G1, G2, then G.
- G1 needs an accompanying driver with four or more years of experience, zero alcohol, and no driving from midnight to 5 a.m. or on 400-series highways.
- G2 allows solo driving but keeps zero alcohol and some night passenger limits for young drivers.
- An approved beginner driver education course (BDE) of 40 hours or more cuts the G1 wait from twelve to eight months.
